Vesicle Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 8, 2026

Vesicle is a one-to-one messenger without a message server. This policy explains what the app does with your data. The short answer: we do not collect it, and our small service sees only what is listed below.

What we collect

Nothing. Vesicle has no accounts, no phone numbers and no sign-up. The app contains no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it does not send crash reports or usage data to us or to anyone else.

Your messages and contacts

A contact is added only in person, by scanning a QR code for that one pair. The camera is used only to read that code; no images are stored or sent. Your device name is not shared with your contact: each person names their own contacts. Each pair has its own keys, created on your devices. Messages, contacts and history are stored only on your device. Vesicle never uploads your address book.

How messages travel

There is no message server. A message is sealed into a capsule that only the recipient can open, and it travels to the recipient through the devices of other Vesicle users. Those devices can see only the sealed capsule: who it is for, how long it lives and how large it is. They cannot read the text, see your keys or learn your contacts.

Nearby devices find each other over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth (this is why the app asks for local network access); over the internet, devices connect directly only with your own contacts.

Using Vesicle means your device also carries other people's sealed capsules. In the settings you choose how much storage and battery this may use. You cannot read what you carry.

Rendezvous service and doorbell

A small service helps two of your devices on different networks find each other. It runs on Yandex Cloud, with a fallback on Cloudflare. Your messages never pass through it.

- Rendezvous. A device leaves a note saying "I am at this address now", encrypted with your pair's key, so the service cannot read it. The note lives for 5 minutes. The service sees the IP address and time of each request but does not store them in the note. - Doorbell. When someone writes to you while Vesicle is closed, the service sends a visible "New message" notification through the Apple Push Notification service. To do this it stores your device's push token under a separate label for each pair, if "Doorbell" is on in settings. So the service may know how many pairs a device has and who rings whom, but not names, text or keys. Turn the doorbell off or delete a pair and the registration is removed.

The notification does not contain the message text or the sender's name. Adding the contact's name on the phone itself will come later. Vesicle never uses silent notifications.

Test builds

Test builds send real messages as described above. They may include a simulation mode that runs only on your device and sends nothing.

Children

Vesicle is not directed at children under 13.

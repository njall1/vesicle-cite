Vesicle Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 9, 2026

Vesicle is a one-to-one messenger with end-to-end encryption. This policy explains what the app does with your data. The short answer: we do not collect it, and our small service sees only what is listed below.

What we collect

Nothing. Vesicle has no accounts, no phone numbers and no sign-up. The app contains no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it does not send crash reports or usage data to us or to anyone else.

Your messages and contacts

A contact is added only in person, by scanning a QR code for that one pair. The camera is used only to read that code; no images are stored or sent. Your device name from the settings is part of your QR code and is sent to your contacts so that the chat has a recognisable name on their side; anyone can rename a contact on their own device. Each pair has its own keys, created on your devices. Messages, contacts and history are stored only on your device. Vesicle never uploads your address book.

How messages travel

A message is sealed into a capsule that only the recipient can open. The capsule then takes the first available route: directly, when you are nearby (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth; this is why the app asks for local network access) or both have the app open; otherwise through our service's mailbox (below); without internet, through the devices of other Vesicle users nearby. Everyone who carries a capsule sees only the capsule itself: who it is for, how long it lives and how large it is. They cannot read the text, see your keys or learn your contacts.

Using Vesicle means your device also carries other people's sealed capsules. In the settings you choose how much storage and battery this may use. You cannot read what you carry.

Our service: mailbox, doorbell and rendezvous

A small service runs on Yandex Cloud, with a fallback on Cloudflare. It cannot read your messages: it has no keys.

- Mailbox. When your contact is offline, sealed capsules wait in the service's mailbox until their app collects them, for at most one day. The mailbox stores the recipient label (16 random bytes for this pair), the capsules, a check value of the pair secret (so the service hands capsules only to your contact) and the time of writing. A copy of each capsule is kept for the same day by the Cloudflare fallback, so that a message arrives even when Yandex is unreachable for the recipient; Cloudflare sees the same label, size, time and check value. The service sees the IP address and time of each request but does not store them in the mailbox. - Rendezvous. When a contact is added by QR, the scanner's answer is left on the service for a day, encrypted with a key from the QR code, so the service cannot read it. - Doorbell. When a message for you is put into the mailbox, the service sends a visible "New message" notification through the Apple Push Notification service. To do this it stores your device's push token under a separate label for each pair, if "Doorbell" is on in settings. So the service may know how many pairs a device has and who writes to whom, but not names, text or keys. The notification carries the sender's addresses for a direct connection, encrypted. Turn the doorbell off or delete a pair and the registration is removed.

The notification does not contain the message text or the sender's name. Adding the contact's name on the phone itself will come later. Vesicle never uses silent notifications.

Test builds

Test builds (TestFlight) send real messages as described above. In addition, test builds with the "Admin" section keep a network journal: connection events (which channel came up, network and NAT type, how long a capsule took, service responses) with the device name and a random install identifier. The journal contains no message text, keys or IP addresses. It is sent to our service and kept for 30 days; only the developer can read it, with a separate key. The App Store version has no journal.

Children

Vesicle is not directed at children under 13.
